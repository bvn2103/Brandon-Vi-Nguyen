# Perfect competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant 10 tomato beds, 20 carrot beds, and 30 mesclun beds, sixty beds in total, for a season profit of $42,762.

## Why

1. **Tomatoes stop because the money says stop.** They pay the most per bed, and they also climb the fastest: each extra tomato bed makes every tomato bed 10% hungrier for labor, four times the rise on carrots and eight times the rise on mesclun. They still sell for $8,800 a bed, and cost climbs with walking, pests, and harvest crush, so bed 10 nets $551 and bed 11 costs $9,391 and loses $591. The $8,800 crop runs into its own wall at bed 10 while 20 are still allowed.

2. **Carrots and mesclun stop the other way.** She hit their bed caps and would still make $352 on one more carrot bed and $246 on one more mesclun bed. Those two caps are the only limits she is actually pressed against.

3. **The leftover capacity is worth nothing today.** Three of her limits are slack, and the tomato cap is the surprise: she plants 60 of 64 beds, buys 4,557 temp hours which is 3.16 of the 4 workers allowed, and grows 10 of the 20 tomatoes she could. Paying to raise any of those three buys nothing, because she already stopped short of all of them. The beds and hours she does have left stay idle because the crops that would use them are capped and another tomato bed already loses money. Slack is leftover land and hours with nothing left that pays for itself.

## The judgment call

Break the carrot cap. The next carrot bed is worth $352 and mesclun is only $246, so if she can clear ground for one crop this year, it is carrots. That $352 is what the model says after the bed already exists. It does not count clearing, soil, or the weeks of work to get there, and if that work costs more than $352 she should not break anything.

## What would change my answer

Watch the tomato price. If a bed ever sells for more than $9,391, plant the eleventh one.
